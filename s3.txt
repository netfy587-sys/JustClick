<section class="block base" id="base">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="section-head">
      <h2>It all starts with the same shot</h2>
      <p>Before milk, water, or ice gets involved, almost every drink on this list begins as espresso — just pulled slightly differently.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="grid">
      <div class="base-copy">
        <p>Espresso is made by forcing hot water through finely-ground, tightly-packed coffee at high pressure, producing a small, concentrated shot in around 25–30 seconds. Everything else on the menu is really just espresso plus something else.</p>
        <p>Even espresso itself has variants, depending only on how much water passes through the same dose of grounds:</p>
        <div class="base-trio">
          <div>
            <h4>Ristretto</h4>
            <p>"Restricted" — less water through the same grounds, for a shorter, more concentrated, syrupy shot.</p>
          </div>
          <div>
            <h4>Espresso</h4>
            <p>The standard shot — the baseline that every other drink on this page is built from.</p>
          </div>
          <div>
            <h4>Lungo</h4>
            <p>"Long" — more water through the same grounds, for a larger, lighter, slightly more bitter shot.</p>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="base-media">
        <figure>
          <img src="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/2014_0508_Caffe_macchiato.jpg" alt="A small espresso-based drink served with a glass of water, a traditional Italian pairing">
          <figcaption>Espresso is traditionally served with a glass of water on the side, to reset the palate.</figcaption>
        </figure>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="milk-family" id="milk">
  <div class="wrap block">
    <div class="section-head">
      <h2>The milk &amp; foam family</h2>
      <p>These drinks all combine espresso with steamed milk — the differences are entirely about proportion: how much milk, how much foam, and how thick that foam is.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="drink-row">
      <div class="drink-media">
        <img src="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Cappucino-bangalore-india.jpg" alt="A cappuccino with a thick layer of milk foam on top">
      </div>
      <div class="drink-copy">
        <h3>Cappuccino</h3>
        <p class="ratio">Roughly equal parts espresso, steamed milk, and thick milk foam</p>
        <p>The three components sit in near-equal thirds, topped with a deep, spoonable layer of foam — sometimes dusted with cocoa. In Italy, it's culturally treated as a morning-only order, rarely drunk after a meal.</p>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="drink-row reverse">
      <div class="drink-media">
        <img src="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Latte_art.jpg" alt="Latte art poured into a cup of coffee with steamed milk">
      </div>
      <div class="drink-copy">
        <h3>Latte</h3>
        <p class="ratio">A single shot of espresso, a large volume of steamed milk, a thin layer of foam</p>
        <p>The mildest and milkiest of the espresso drinks — mostly steamed milk, with just enough foam on top to hold a poured pattern. It's the usual canvas for latte art.</p>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="drink-row">
      <div class="drink-media">
        <img src="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Flat_white.jpg" alt="A flat white coffee with a thin layer of microfoam">
      </div>
      <div class="drink-copy">
        <h3>Flat white</h3>
        <p class="ratio">A stronger shot-to-milk ratio than a latte, with thin "microfoam" instead of thick foam</p>
        <p>Shorter and stronger than a latte, with velvety microfoam rather than a thick foam cap — hence "flat." Café owners in both Australia and New Zealand claim its invention.</p>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="drink-row reverse">
      <div class="drink-media">
        <img src="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Caff%C3%A8_macchiato.jpg" alt="An espresso macchiato with just a small dollop of foamed milk on top">
      </div>
      <div class="drink-copy">
        <h3>Macchiato</h3>
        <p class="ratio">A shot of espresso "marked" with just a spoonful of foamed milk</p>
        <p>The opposite ratio of a latte — almost all espresso, with only a small dollop of milk foam on top. "Macchiato" is Italian for "stained" or "marked."</p>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="drink-row">
      <div class="drink-media">
        <img src="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/White_chocolate_mocha_coffee.jpg" alt="A mocha coffee drink with chocolate and steamed milk">
      </div>
      <div class="drink-copy">
        <h3>Mocha</h3>
        <p class="ratio">A latte with chocolate syrup or cocoa added</p>
        <p>Essentially a latte with chocolate stirred in, landing somewhere between a coffee and a dessert. The name comes from Mocha, a Yemeni port city historically central to the early coffee trade.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="block two-col-feature black-coffee" id="black">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="section-head">
      <h2>The straight black family</h2>
      <p>No milk at all — just espresso, stretched with hot water to change its strength and volume.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="grid">
      <div class="feat-media">
        <img src="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Americano.jpg" alt="A cup of caffe americano, espresso diluted with hot water">
      </div>
      <div class="feat-copy">
        <h3>Americano</h3>
        <p>Hot water is added to one or two shots of espresso, usually at around a 1:3 or 1:4 ratio of espresso to water. The result keeps espresso's complex flavour but in a lighter, larger cup — close in strength to drip coffee.</p>
        <p>Pour the water in first, and the espresso stays on top, keeping the crema visible — that version is usually called a "long black" instead.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="block two-col-feature dessert" id="dessert">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="section-head">
      <h2>When coffee becomes dessert</h2>
      <p>Not every espresso drink is meant to be sipped slowly through a morning — some are built for the end of a meal.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="grid reverse">
      <div class="feat-media">
        <img src="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Affogato_al_Caffe.jpg" alt="A scoop of vanilla gelato with a hot shot of espresso poured over it, an affogato">
      </div>
      <div class="feat-copy">
        <h3>Affogato</h3>
        <p>A scoop of vanilla gelato or ice cream, "drowned" — which is what affogato means in Italian — under a hot shot of espresso poured right at the table. Part drink, part dessert, and eaten with a spoon before it melts.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>
